# Antihydrogen Measurements

This folder is a compact working registry for antihydrogen and related anti-atom measurements that look useful for CGM-style coordinate tests.

Files:

- [measurements.csv](F:\Development\science\data\antihydrogen\measurements.csv)
- `source_docs/`

What is most valuable for CGM tests:

- transition frequencies or wavelengths
- trapping / confinement times
- gravity observables expressed as ratios to `g`
- beam temperatures, intensities, and state fractions

Why these are useful:

- spectroscopy gives clean dimensionful observables with precise hydrogen-side counterparts
- gravity gives a non-spectral observable in a different physical channel
- beam properties may reveal compact residual structure in kinematics and state composition

Practical cautions:

- some CERN pages are news summaries rather than the full paper
- the 2017 Nature PDF link did not download cleanly in this workspace, so the registry uses the official ALPHA publication page for that result
- not every number here has the same evidential strength; spectroscopy is much cleaner than beam composition estimates

Good next CGM tests:

1. Map the antihydrogen hyperfine splitting and the 1S-2S transition to the same compact ruler used for hydrogen-side observables.
2. Compare antihydrogen gravity `a_gbar / g` against compact residual candidates without mixing it into the spectroscopy sector.
3. Track beam temperature and ground-state fraction separately from atomic transition data.
