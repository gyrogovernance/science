## Scope

This analysis reads DNA as data on hQVM carrier objects (bytes, fold P, Omega = U x V, chirality shells, K4 gauge, dual charts) and derives biological symmetries (reverse-complement, the genetic code partition, strand structure, translation dynamics) as theorems or classified structures on those objects. The central question is whether the standard genetic code and the observed properties of real genomes are constrained by the same CGM geometry that constrains spacetime, particle masses, and the gravitational coupling. The lift and the derived grammar define a deterministic compile from sequence to carrier features; classification of genes, intervals, codes, and variant channels is the application of that compile to labeled genomic catalogs.

### Research scope (script 8 onward)

Direction of argument is one-way: CGM axioms to hQVM objects to genomic theorems and signals. Reverse fishing (metric first, story after) is out of scope. CGM is scale-agnostic: CS, UNA, ONA, BU govern all phenomena; finding the four stages in a byte or codon does not exhaust them — the same palindromic ladder must be readable at promoter, ORF, replichore, and organism scales. A complete genomic reading addresses three necessities forced by Gravity_Note: topology (fold, wall, percolation, homology), temporality (depth-4 closure, Ingress memory, path-ordered holonomy), and freedoms (the 1-3-6 degrees-of-freedom ladder: UNA rotation, ONA rotation plus translation, SE(3)-sized six-bit transport). Claim strength uses four levels: level 1 necessary genomic architecture (bipartite carrier, complementarity, ancestry-preserving mutation, closure, memory), level 2 constrained code architecture (triplets, fibers, stop boundary, RC covariance, fold-plane wall), level 3 historically selected realization (the exact table up to Aff_S6 moduli), level 4 organism occupation (usage, regulation, splice; needs biological boundary conditions). The standard code is argued as a CGM-constrained attractor whose four known NCBI wall breaches are surgical moves at classifiable BU positions — not as "uniquely forced" until Open #2 enumeration completes. When a work session lacks a clear image of what to do, pivot by re-reading Analysis_Gravity_Note.md sections 1-2 with Appendix A, Analysis_Holonomy.md sections 6, 9, 15-17, hQVM_Specs_Formalism.md, Wavefunction T7-T9, and Theorems 8-11 with Notes 14-16 here — before writing any new probe.

## Theory Notes

### Context

#### Nucleotide encoding and byte stream

Nucleotides are labeled by 2 bits. There are 24 affine encodings (GL(2,2) x Z2^2) falling into three metric orbits of 8 each. The elementary_axes orbit has WC polarity weight-3 (010101 or 101010). The pair_inversion orbit has WC polarity 111111, which is the antipode, and is fold-compatible. The other orbit has the same weight-3 polarities as elementary.

A codon is three bases and produces a 6-bit payload in GF(2)^6. An hQVM byte is family (K4, 2 bits) plus micro-payload (6 bits). The canonical genomic stream converts a DNA string to overlapping 4-mers and packs each 4-mer as one hQVM byte.

Two RC maps operate on bytes. The kinematic 4-mer RC is the DNA reverse-complement of the packed 4-mer. The payload-RC is the codon-RC on the 6-bit micro with family bits held fixed. These are distinct maps. They agree on 0 of 256 bytes. Kinematic RC flips the family bits on 192 of 256 4-mers because the last base of the 4-mer moves into the family slot. This disagreement is the locus where family and context exchange roles with codon payload.

#### Codon, dicodon, and byte identification

The codon is a point of the constitutional horizon H with |H| = 64. The adjacent pair of codons is a point of Omega = U x V, which is the reading frame. The chirality register chi = q(c_i) XOR q(c_{i+1}) is the information velocity of the message. Translation is the step_uv walk on Omega: the P-site is the record face U (past codon), the A-site is the active face V (incoming codon), and peptidyl transfer realizes the complement-and-swap gyration.

The three codon positions map to the three interior stage-pairs of the byte palindrome. Wobble (position 2) occupies payload bits 0 and 1 at stages UNA and ONA, which is the forward half and active frame. Middle (position 1) occupies payload bits 2 and 3 at stages BU and BU, which is the central fold. First (position 0) occupies payload bits 4 and 5 at stages ONA and UNA, which is the reverse half and record frame. The codon is a transverse cut of the stage palindrome.

#### Reverse complement layers

Four layers of reverse-complement action operate from carrier to chirality. Layer A is kinematic and carrier-level: the RC of the sequence byte stream is the reverse of the letterwise kinematic-RC of forward bytes. Layer B is on Omega signatures under payload-RC with residual Z_n (Theorem 6). Layer C is polarity and fold: on payload GF(2)^6, fold and payload-RC commute on pair_inversion (Theorem 4). Layer D is chirality: the residual q(RC_kin(w)) XOR q(w) is the XOR of letterwise lambda values, which lie in W (Theorem 5).

#### hQVM objects bound to genomic observables

The kernel provides the following objects as genomic observables. The byte has family(2) and payload(6) with q6 transport class, chi register, and 7-shell ladder. Walsh and Krawtchouk transforms diagonalize all radial and additive processes. Omega = U x V carries the codon and dicodon identification. On the pair_inversion chart, the affine hull ranks of synonymous fibers are as follows. Size-4 complete boxes have affine rank 2. Size-2 pairs have affine rank 1. Size-1 fibers have affine rank 0. Affine hull rank is the chart-invariant size measure of a codon set. Null N3, which preserves fiber geometry, recovers beta1 = 27. Nulls N1, N2, and N4 do not.

The affine structure of the fibers describes their internal geometry. The next result describes how these fibers are labeled by the family gauge.

#### Theorem 2: Family gauge sheet and serine complementarity

The two family bits of a byte are exactly (eps_a, eps_b), the two boundary polarities that switch the all-1 translations on U and V. Amino acid is a function of payload only. The genetic code is the quotient of the 8-bit word by this K4 sheet. Kinematic RC mixes the sheet because 4-mer reversal sends interior bits into the family slots (Theorem 5).

Length-1 Omega signatures of (family, codon) are injective for 19 of 20 amino acids. Serine is the exception with 16 signatures among 24 sheet-copies. Its two disconnected fibers TCN and AGY are Watson-Crick complements of each other: AGC pairs with TCG and AGT pairs with TCA. Their signatures coincide after the eps_b sheet flip (family XOR 2). This is the unique disconnected fiber (Theorem 1) and the unique signature collision.

The family gauge determines how meaning is encoded. The boundary of the semantic space is the stop codon set, whose topology we now examine.

#### Theorem 4: Codon reverse-complement factorization

Codon reverse-complement on GF(2)^6 factors as RC(q) = R_block(q) XOR delta, where R_block reverses the three 2-bit codon positions (universal across all 24 charts) and delta is the Watson-Crick polarity of the chart. Fold action on the 6-bit payload is bit-reversal R_bit (reverse indices 0 through 5). Fold and the polarity translation T_delta commute if and only if R_bit(delta) equals delta.

On pair_inversion (8 of 8 charts), delta = 111111, the antipode, and fold composed with payload-RC commutes on 2048 of 2048 cases. On elementary and other (16 charts), delta has weight-3 and the commutation count is 0 of 2048. On pair_inversion, Watson-Crick polarity is the antipode of the 6-cube, which is the same polarity the kernel fold uses.

The factorization separates RC into a universal linear part and a chart-dependent polarity. The next theorem examines what happens when the kinematic 4-mer RC, rather than the payload-RC, is applied.

#### Theorem 5: Kinematic RC residual and subspace W

The chirality residual of kinematic RC is lambda(b) = q(b) XOR q(RC_kin(b)). This lies in a 4-dimensional linear subspace W of GF(2)^6, the same for all 24 encodings. On words, q(RC(w)) XOR q(w) equals the XOR of letterwise lambda(b_i), which is exactly additive.

W has the explicit characterization W = {x in GF(2)^6 : x_1 = x_3 and x_2 = x_4}. This has rank 4, 16 vectors, and contains 0 and the antipode 111111. The two equations are the annihilator masks 001010 (bit1 + bit3) and 010100 (bit2 + bit4). W is the fix-space of swapping the two interior 2-bit bases of a 4-mer payload, which are the two middle nucleotides of the packed 4-mer. Fold bit-reversal preserves W. Codon-position block-reverse sends W outside itself.

The four free coordinates are the two endpoint payload bits (bit0, bit5) and the two diagonal bits of the interior 2x2 (bit1=bit3, bit2=bit4). These are the 4 of 6 degrees of freedom that the kinematic residual occupies.

The kinematic residual is confined to W. The next theorem shows that the payload-RC, by contrast, acts cleanly on Omega signatures.

#### Theorem 6: Payload-RC anti-action on Omega signatures

Payload-RC (family held fixed, codon-RC on the 6-bit micro), reversed as a word, is a function of the Omega signature. For each byte b = (family, payload), the kinematic 4-mer RC defines a map q_kin on bytes.
