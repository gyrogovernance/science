PART 1 - SO(3) FROM ITS OWN THEORY (28 checks)
=================================================================
  1. Group axioms: closure, associativity, inverse; structure
     constants; Jacobi identity (integrability of the group law)
  2. Root system A1: Cartan subalgebra, roots {+i,-i}, Weyl group
     W(A1) = Z/2 (the discrete skeleton of the double cover)
  3. exp/log dictionary: surjectivity; exact BCH closed form
     (Engo 2001), 1e-15 on 400 random pairs; series-truncation
     comparison (series needs ||X||+||Y|| < log 2)
  4. Representation theory: dim D^j = 2j+1; character orthogonality
     (integer j on SO(3)); Clebsch-Gordan via characters
     chi_j1 * chi_j2 = sum_J chi_J (exact)
  5. Harmonic analysis: L^2(S^2) fixed-l irreducibility (rotated
     Y_lm stays in V_l); addition theorem; Peter-Weyl on SO(3)
     (j=1, adjoint) and on SU(2) (j=1/2) - with the correct subtlety
     that the principal-branch lift R->U is NOT a homomorphism,
     hence spinors need the double cover
  6. Geometry: geodesics = one-parameter subgroups; sectional
     curvature via geodesic-square holonomy; Euler top on T*SO(3)
     with E and |L| conserved to 1e-12 (6-DoF rigid body)
  7. Topology: cellular homology of RP^3 = SO(3):
     H*(Z) = {Z, Z/2, 0, Z}, H*(Z/2) = Z/2[c]/(c^4)
  8. Random rotations: Haar density f(theta) = (2/pi) sin^2(theta/2),
     chi2 fit; eigenvalue statistics E[tr R] = 0

KERNEL AS A ROTATION-COMPOSITION ENGINE (benchmark, Part 2 sec. F)
=================================================================
  Composition of rotations = 'matrix multiplication' of the finite
  group G. Vectorized bitwise path (compose_sig_batch, 13-bit packed
  signatures, XOR/select only):
    - rotation composition: 74.4 M/s vs 15.7 M/s float 3x3 BLAS
      = 4.7x faster; vs scipy Rotation ~ 30x faster
    - state application: 94.1 M/s via 4.2 MB L3-resident transition
      table vs 49.7 M/s float 3x3 @ v = 1.9x faster
  Why: 13 bits pack per uint16 (4 per 64-bit register), ~4 integer
  ops instead of 27 float multiply-adds; exact (no FP, no
  normalization drift, no branch cuts).
  Honest limits: composes the FINITE group G only - not general
  matrix multiplication, not continuous-angle SO(3). The study's
  pure-Python compose path is ~0.2 M/s (object overhead); the
  vectorized path is the engine-grade implementation.
